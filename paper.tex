\begin{abstract}
We present an effective field theory (EFT) framework for testing scale--dependent modifications of static, spherically symmetric spacetime geometries, motivated by candidate quantum--gravity scenarios. Within a weak--field expansion we construct three benchmark metric ans\"atze (Yukawa--type, power--law, and RG--improved) that capture leading deviations from General Relativity (GR) induced by running couplings, discrete microscopic structure or higher--curvature operators. For each ansatz we provide explicit mappings to parameterized post--Newtonian (PPN) parameters and to the parameterized post--Einsteinian (ppE) coefficients entering gravitational--wave phasing, and derive analytic expressions for perihelion precession, light deflection and Shapiro time delay. As a worked example we obtain Gaia--like forecast constraints on a Yukawa correction to the solar potential, including a discussion of dominant systematics. A public Python toolkit implementing all observables and basic validation tests is provided and archived at a citable Zenodo DOI. This framework provides tools for systematic searches within a representative set of scale--dependent parameterizations motivated by quantum gravity across solar--system, binary--pulsar and gravitational--wave data, while explicitly stating the limitations of the weak--field truncation and leaving detailed stability analyses and full Bayesian global fits to follow--up work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

General Relativity (GR) has passed an impressive battery of experimental tests over more than a century, from solar--system experiments to binary pulsars and gravitational waves \cite{Will2014,Will2018}. At the same time, there are compelling theoretical reasons to expect that GR must eventually be modified or extended: as an effective field theory (EFT) it is non--renormalizable in the ultraviolet (UV) \cite{Donoghue1994,Burgess2004}, and it is challenged by the cosmological constant problem, dark matter and dark energy \cite{Clifton2012,Joyce2015}. This motivates a broad program of precision tests of gravity, designed to probe possible deviations from GR across multiple scales and environments.

A key lesson from both EFT and candidate quantum--gravity (QG) approaches is that spacetime geometry may become \emph{scale--dependent} in a nontrivial way. In asymptotically safe gravity the Newton coupling $G(k)$ becomes a running coupling under the renormalization group (RG) \cite{ReuterSaueressig2012}; in loop quantum gravity (LQG) discrete area spectra and polymer quantization suggest an effective coarse--graining of geometry \cite{Rovelli2004,AshtekarBojowald2005}; in noncommutative geometry, the spectral triple formalism introduces a fundamental length scale \cite{Connes1994,SeibergWitten1999,Nicolini2006,Ansoldi2008}. In all these cases one expects, at least in principle, that classical metric coefficients acquire a nontrivial dependence on an effective scale parameter.

In phenomenological applications, however, there is an important conceptual subtlety: the word ``scale'' can mean different things:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item the RG scale $k$ in a Wilsonian EFT, defined in momentum space;
\item a geometric or curvature scale, such as a radius $r$ or an invariant like $K=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$;
\item an experimental resolution scale $L_{\rm res}$, related to the finite size of probes or detectors.
\end{itemize}
Our point of view in this work is deliberately pragmatic. We construct weak--field, static, spherically symmetric metric ans\"atze which explicitly encode a radius--dependent deviation from the Schwarzschild solution, and we regard these as phenomenological parameterizations of possible scale--dependent geometries. Where relevant, we indicate how such ans\"atze may arise from or be inspired by more microscopic QG scenarios, but we do not attempt to derive them from a complete UV theory.

The primary goals of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item to define a set of benchmark scale--dependent metric ans\"atze that are sufficiently general to capture a broad class of QG--motivated corrections, yet simple enough to be mapped onto standard phenomenological frameworks such as PPN and ppE;
\item to derive explicit expressions for observables such as perihelion precession, light deflection and Shapiro time delay, and to summarize the sensitivity of a variety of probes (solar system, binary pulsars, gravitational waves, laboratory experiments);
\item to provide a reproducible Python toolkit implementing these ans\"atze and observables, with basic validation tests and schematic combined--constraint plots, archived at a permanent Zenodo DOI;
\item to clarify the limitations of the weak--field truncation, the role of energy conditions and stability, and the scheme dependence inherent in RG--improved ans\"atze.
\end{enumerate}

This is therefore a \emph{framework} paper. We focus on the structure of scale--dependent parameterizations and their mapping to observables. Detailed stability analyses of specific modified--gravity theories, and full Bayesian global fits to data, are left to companion work.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.~\ref{sec:metric-ansatze} we introduce the general weak--field metric parameterization and define three benchmark ans\"atze (Yukawa, power--law, RG--improved), together with a table of notation. Section~\ref{sec:ppn} connects these ans\"atze to the parameterized post--Newtonian formalism, introduces a scale--dependent effective $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)$ and discusses a minimal versus an extended modeling choice. In Sec.~\ref{sec:observables} we derive expressions for perihelion precession, light deflection and Shapiro time delay and briefly summarize the mapping to the ppE framework for gravitational waves. Section~\ref{sec:multiprobe} discusses a multi--probe strategy and summarizes existing bounds in two tables. Section~\ref{sec:gaia} presents a Gaia--like forecast for Yukawa corrections to the solar potential, including dominant systematics. In Sec.~\ref{sec:energy} we analyze weak energy conditions in the weak--field approximation and discuss their limitations as diagnostic tools. Section~\ref{sec:degeneracies} comments on degeneracies among ans\"atze and RG scheme dependence. We conclude in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}. Appendix~A contains some technical details on effective energy densities, Appendix~B sketches the derivation of the Yukawa deflection integral, and Appendix~C briefly summarizes the Python toolkit.

\section{Weak--field metric and benchmark ans\"atze}
\label{sec:metric-ansatze}

We consider static, spherically symmetric metrics written in areal--radius gauge,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metric}
ds^2 = -\,A(r)\,c^2 dt^2 + B(r)\,dr^2 + r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\,,
\end{equation}
with $A(r),B(r)\to 1$ as $r\to\infty$. In GR, the exterior solution of a point mass $M$ is Schwarzschild, with
\begin{equation}
A_{\rm GR}(r) = 1 - \frac{2G_0M}{c^2 r}\,, \qquad B_{\rm GR}(r) = \left(1 - \frac{2G_0M}{c^2 r}\right)^{-1},
\end{equation}
where $G_0$ is Newton's constant. In the weak--field regime $G_0M/(c^2r)\ll 1$ we introduce effective potentials $\Phi(r)$ and $\Psi(r)$ via
\begin{equation}
A(r) \simeq 1 + \frac{2\Phi(r)}{c^2}\,, \qquad B(r) \simeq 1 - \frac{2\Psi(r)}{c^2}\,,
\label{eq:PhiPsi-def}
\end{equation}
and we define the positive Newtonian potential magnitude
\begin{equation}
U(r) \equiv \frac{G_0 M}{r} > 0\,, \qquad \Phi_{\rm N}(r) = -U(r)\,.
\end{equation}
In GR one has $\Phi_{\rm GR}(r) = \Psi_{\rm GR}(r) = -U(r)$ at leading order.

We parameterize deviations from GR by
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -U(r)\,\bigl[1 + \delta_\Phi(r)\bigr]\,, \qquad
\Psi(r) = -U(r)\,\bigl[1 + \delta_\Psi(r)\bigr]\,,
\label{eq:deltas}
\end{equation}
with $\delta_\Phi,\delta_\Psi\to 0$ as $r\to\infty$. It is also convenient to define a scale--dependent effective PPN parameter
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\rm eff}(r) \equiv \frac{\Psi(r)}{\Phi(r)} = 
\frac{1+\delta_\Psi(r)}{1+\delta_\Phi(r)}\,,
\label{eq:gamma-eff}
\end{equation}
which reduces to the usual constant PPN parameter $\gamma$ when the ratio is $r$--independent. The Cassini measurement of the Shapiro time delay constrains $\gamma$ at solar--system scales to \cite{BertottiIessTortora2003}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cassini-bound}
\bigl|\gamma - 1\bigr| \lesssim 2.3\times 10^{-5}
\end{equation}
for impact parameters $b\sim 1.6\,R_\odot$ and heliocentric distances of order $1$--$8$\,AU.

\subsection{Benchmark Yukawa ansatz}

A widely used benchmark parameterization of deviations from Newtonian gravity is a Yukawa correction,
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -\,\frac{G_0 M}{r}\Bigl[1 + \alpha_{\rm Y}\,e^{-r/\lambda_{\rm Y}}\Bigr],
\label{eq:yukawa-Phi}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\rm Y}$ is a dimensionless strength and $\lambda_{\rm Y}$ a length scale. For $|\alpha_{\rm Y}|\ll 1$ and $r\ll \lambda_{\rm Y}$ the correction reduces to a small rescaling of $G_0$, while for $r\gg\lambda_{\rm Y}$ it is exponentially suppressed.

For concreteness we will often adopt a ``minimal'' Yukawa ansatz in which the two metric potentials are proportional,
\begin{equation}
\Psi(r) = \gamma_\infty\,\Phi(r)\,,
\label{eq:minimal-ansatz}
\end{equation}
with a constant $\gamma_\infty$ close to unity. In this case $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)\equiv\gamma_\infty$ is exactly constant within the weak--field truncation \eqref{eq:PhiPsi-def}, and the Cassini bound \eqref{eq:cassini-bound} constrains $\gamma_\infty$ directly. In more general models (for example scalar--tensor theories) $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ receive different corrections and $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)$ acquires a mild $r$--dependence even in the weak--field regime; we return to this point in Sec.~\ref{sec:ppn}.

\subsection{Power--law ansatz}

To capture possible long--range tails or fractal--like corrections one may consider a power--law deformation,
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -\,\frac{G_0 M}{r}\Bigl[1 + \epsilon\Bigl(\frac{r}{\ell_s}\Bigr)^\beta\Bigr],
\label{eq:powerlaw-Phi}
\end{equation}
with dimensionless amplitude $\epsilon$, exponent $\beta$ and reference scale $\ell_s$. For $|\epsilon|\ll 1$ and moderate values of $\beta$ this ansatz can approximate a variety of scale--dependent behaviors over a finite range of radii. As in the Yukawa case we may adopt a minimal ansatz $\Psi(r)=\gamma_\infty\Phi(r)$ or a more general extended parameterization with independent deviations in $\Phi$ and $\Psi$.

\subsection{RG--improved ansatz}

In asymptotically safe gravity and other QG approaches, the Newton coupling becomes scale--dependent, $G(k)$, with nontrivial RG flow \cite{ReuterSaueressig2012}. A simple phenomenological way to encode this in a static, spherically symmetric metric is to replace $G_0\to G(r)$ in the Schwarzschild solution, leading to
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -\,\frac{G(r) M}{r}\,,
\end{equation}
with a parameterization such as
\begin{equation}
G(r) = G_0\Bigl[1 + \xi\Bigl(1 + \frac{r}{r_0}\Bigr)^{-n}\Bigr],
\label{eq:G-of-r}
\end{equation}
where $\xi$ controls the amplitude of the running, $n>0$ the fall--off and $r_0$ an effective transition scale. This should be understood as a phenomenological representation of a class of RG--improved metrics rather than as a prediction of any specific truncation. A crucial ambiguity here is the identification of the RG scale $k$ with a geometric scale; prescriptions such as $k\sim 1/r$ or $k^4\sim K$ with $K$ the Kretschmann scalar lead to different $G(r)$ for the same $G(k)$. We return to this ``scheme dependence'' in Sec.~\ref{sec:degeneracies}.

\section{Connection to the PPN framework}
\label{sec:ppn}

In the standard PPN framework \cite{Will2014,Will2018} one writes, in isotropic coordinates and to leading order in $U/c^2$,
\begin{equation}
g_{tt} = -1 + 2\frac{U}{c^2} + 2\beta\frac{U^2}{c^4} + \cdots,\qquad
g_{rr} = 1 + 2\gamma\frac{U}{c^2} + \cdots,
\end{equation}
with constant PPN parameters $\beta,\gamma$ and additional parameters entering non--spherical or time--dependent sectors. For the static, spherically symmetric sector of interest here, the primary parameter constrained by light--propagation experiments is $\gamma$.

In our weak--field parameterization \eqref{eq:PhiPsi-def} and \eqref{eq:deltas}, the effective PPN parameter becomes $r$--dependent:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\rm eff}(r) = \frac{1+\delta_\Psi(r)}{1+\delta_\Phi(r)}\,.
\end{equation}
For the Yukawa ansatz \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi} with the minimal proportionality \eqref{eq:minimal-ansatz} one has $\delta_\Psi(r) = \delta_\Phi(r)$ and hence $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r) \equiv \gamma_\infty$ exactly constant. The Cassini bound \eqref{eq:cassini-bound} then constrains $\gamma_\infty$ at radii sampled by the radio link.

In more general extended ans\"atze one writes
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -U(r)\bigl[1 + \delta_\Phi(r)\bigr],\qquad
\Psi(r) = -U(r)\bigl[1 + \delta_\Psi(r)\bigr], 
\end{equation}
with independent functional forms for $\delta_\Phi,\delta_\Psi$. The Cassini measurement constrains a combination of these evaluated along the light path. In practice, for solar--system tests one may adopt a ``minimal'' modeling choice---constant $\gamma_{\rm eff}$---provided that the variation of $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)$ across the region probed is much smaller than the experimental uncertainty,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\gamma_{\rm eff} \equiv \max_{r\in{\cal R}}\gamma_{\rm eff}(r) - \min_{r\in{\cal R}}\gamma_{\rm eff}(r) \ll 2.3\times 10^{-5},
\end{equation}
where ${\cal R}$ denotes the relevant interval of heliocentric distances. Within the weak--field truncation \eqref{eq:PhiPsi-def}, the minimal Yukawa ansatz satisfies $\Delta\gamma_{\rm eff}=0$ exactly; post--Newtonian corrections and more general models will induce small scale dependence which can be captured in the extended ansatz.

\section{Key observables: perihelion precession, light deflection and Shapiro delay}
\label{sec:observables}

In this section we summarize the expressions for three classical observables: perihelion precession of bound orbits, light deflection and Shapiro time delay. Detailed derivations can be found in standard references \cite{Wald1984,Will2014,AdkinsMcDonnell2007} and are adapted to our ans\"atze here.

\subsection{Perihelion precession}

Consider a test particle of negligible mass moving in the equatorial plane of the metric \eqref{eq:metric}. In the weak--field, slow--motion regime the secular precession of the perihelion due to a small perturbation $\delta\Phi(r)$ of the Newtonian potential can be written as \cite{AdkinsMcDonnell2007}
\begin{equation}
\Delta\varpi \simeq -\,\frac{1}{n a^2 e}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial e}\Bigl\langle \delta\Phi\bigl(r(f)\bigr)\Bigr\rangle_f,
\label{eq:perihelion-general}
\end{equation}
where angle brackets denote the orbital average over true anomaly $f$,
\begin{equation}
\langle X\rangle_f \equiv \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} X(f)\,df,
\end{equation}
and the Keplerian orbit is
\begin{equation}
r(f) = \frac{a\,(1-e^2)}{1+e\cos f}.
\end{equation}
For the Yukawa ansatz \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi} the perturbation relative to the Newtonian potential is
\begin{equation}
\delta\Phi(r) = -\,\frac{G_0 M}{r}\,\alpha_{\rm Y}\,e^{-r/\lambda_{\rm Y}}.
\end{equation}
The integral \eqref{eq:perihelion-general} does not admit a simple closed--form expression in general, but it can be evaluated numerically with high accuracy for given $(\alpha_{\rm Y},\lambda_{\rm Y})$ and $(a,e)$. Our Python toolkit implements this using adaptive quadrature and Richardson extrapolation for the eccentricity derivative; see Appendix~C.

\subsection{Light deflection}

In the metric \eqref{eq:metric} with weak--field potentials \eqref{eq:PhiPsi-def}, the deflection angle of a light ray with impact parameter $b$ can be written as (see, e.g., \cite{Will2014,Wald1984} and Appendix~B)
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}(b) \simeq \frac{2}{c^2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} 
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigl[\Phi(r)+\Psi(r)\bigr]\frac{b\,dz}{r} \,,
\qquad r = \sqrt{b^2 + z^2}.
\label{eq:deflection-general}
\end{equation}
For GR one has $\Phi=\Psi=-U(r)$ and this yields the familiar result
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}(b) = \frac{4 G_0 M}{c^2 b}.
\end{equation}
In the minimal Yukawa ansatz \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi}--\eqref{eq:minimal-ansatz}, light deflection depends on the combination $(1+\gamma_\infty)\Phi(r)$, and one may define an effective scale--dependent $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)$ by comparing the full integral \eqref{eq:deflection-general} to the GR value. In the small--$\alpha_{\rm Y}$ regime deviations are suppressed roughly by $|\alpha_{\rm Y}|\,e^{-b/\lambda_{\rm Y}}$ relative to GR, modulo geometric factors.

Our toolkit implements \eqref{eq:deflection-general} both analytically in the GR limit and numerically for general Yukawa parameters. A validation test checks that the numerical result agrees with the analytic GR expression when $\alpha_{\rm Y}\to 0$ and $\gamma_\infty\to 1$.

\subsection{Shapiro time delay}

The Shapiro time delay for a light ray propagating in a static, spherically symmetric metric depends on the same combination $\Phi+\Psi$ that enters light deflection \cite{Will2014}. For a nearly radial trajectory with closest approach $b$ one finds, to leading order,
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\rm Shapiro} \simeq -\frac{1}{c^3}\int_{\rm path} \bigl[\Phi(r)+\Psi(r)\bigr]\, d\ell\,,
\end{equation}
where $d\ell$ is the Euclidean line element along the path. For GR this yields the standard logarithmic dependence on the endpoints and impact parameter; for scale--dependent ans\"atze the deviations can be expressed in terms of $\delta_\Phi,\delta_\Psi$ evaluated along the path. The Cassini constraint \eqref{eq:cassini-bound} may be rephrased as a bound on $\gamma_{\rm eff}(r)$ at heliocentric distances and impact parameters probed by the radio link.

\subsection{Connection to ppE}
\label{sec:ppe}

Parameterized post--Einsteinian (ppE) frameworks \cite{YunesPretorius2009,YunesSiemens2013} describe deviations from GR in the Fourier--domain gravitational--wave (GW) phase, for example
\begin{equation}
\Psi(f) = \Psi_{\rm GR}(f) + \beta_{\rm ppE}\, u^b, \qquad 
u = (\pi {\cal M} f)^{1/3},
\end{equation}
where ${\cal M}$ is the chirp mass and $(\beta_{\rm ppE},b)$ encode the amplitude and PN order of deviations. In general, mapping a given metric ansatz to ppE coefficients requires a calculation of the binary binding energy and GW luminosity in the modified theory, and thus depends on UV--complete dynamics, including possible extra degrees of freedom.

In this framework paper we restrict ourselves to outlining the procedure and to a simple worked example. For a Yukawa correction with $\lambda_{\rm Y}\gg r_{\rm orb}$, the conservative dynamics can be approximated by an effective rescaling $G_0\to G_0(1+\alpha_{\rm Y})$ over the orbital separation $r_{\rm orb}$, leading to a small shift in the chirp mass inferred under the assumption of GR. In this limit the phasing is degenerate with a change of intrinsic parameters, and no clean ppE deviation appears. Genuine ppE--type phase corrections arise when $\lambda_{\rm Y}\sim r_{\rm orb}$ and/or when additional fields contribute to radiation; explicit expressions then become model--dependent. We therefore treat the ppE connection here as a structural link and defer explicit model--specific mappings to future work.

\section{Gaia--like forecast for Yukawa corrections}
\label{sec:gaia}

To illustrate how the framework can be used in practice we consider a simplified Gaia--like forecast for Yukawa corrections to the solar potential. We assume that the dominant effect on the astrometric positions of background sources near the solar limb arises from modified light deflection in the static metric with $\Phi(r)$ of the form \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi} and $\Psi(r)=\gamma_\infty\Phi(r)$.

The GR deflection angle at impact parameter $b$ is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}(b) = \frac{4G_0M_\odot}{c^2 b}\,.
\end{equation}
In the minimal Yukawa ansatz the corrected angle can be written as
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}(b) \simeq \hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}(b)\,\bigl[1 + \delta_{\rm Y}(b;\alpha_{\rm Y},\lambda_{\rm Y})\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{\rm Y}(b)$ encodes the relative change induced by the Yukawa factor in the integral \eqref{eq:deflection-general}. For $|\alpha_{\rm Y}|\ll 1$ and $b\sim R_\odot$ one may approximate $\delta_{\rm Y}(b)\sim \alpha_{\rm Y} F(b/\lambda_{\rm Y})$ with $F$ an ${\cal O}(1)$ function.

For bright sources ($G<15$\,mag) at impact parameters $b\sim R_\odot$, the single--epoch along--scan astrometric uncertainty in Gaia DR3 is of order $\sigma_{\rm pos}\sim 30\,\mu{\rm as}$ \cite{GaiaDR3}. Over $N_{\rm obs}\sim 70$ independent epochs spanning five years, the statistical uncertainty on the deflection angle scales as
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\hat{\alpha}}(b)\sim \frac{\sigma_{\rm pos}}{\sqrt{N_{\rm obs}}}\sim 4\,\mu{\rm as},
\end{equation}
ignoring correlations and systematics. Comparing to the GR deflection at $b\sim R_\odot$, $\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}\sim 1.75$\,arcsec, this corresponds to a fractional sensitivity of order
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sigma_{\hat{\alpha}}}{\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}} \sim 10^{-3}\,.
\end{equation}
Assuming $\delta_{\rm Y}(b)\approx \alpha_{\rm Y}F(b/\lambda_{\rm Y})$ with $F\sim 1$, this yields an indicative bound $|\alpha_{\rm Y}|\lesssim 10^{-3}$ for $\lambda_{\rm Y}$ in the range $0.01$--$1$\,AU.

A more rigorous treatment requires accounting for Gaia's scanning law, source distribution, correlations between along--scan and across--scan measurements, and systematics such as the solar quadrupole $J_2$, planetary deflections and instrumental effects. As a simple example, the contribution to the deflection from the solar quadrupole scales as \cite{Will2014}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}_{J_2}(b) \sim J_2\,\frac{R_\odot^2}{b^2}\,\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}(b),
\end{equation}
which for $J_2\sim 2\times 10^{-7}$ and $b\sim R_\odot$ gives $|\hat{\alpha}_{J_2}|\sim 10^{-3}\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}$, comparable to the statistical sensitivity. However, $J_2$ can be constrained independently and its angular dependence differs from that of an isotropic Yukawa correction, so the two effects can in principle be disentangled.

Our forecast should therefore be regarded as an order--of--magnitude indication of the potential of Gaia--like astrometry to constrain Yukawa--type deviations, rather than as a full analysis. A realistic Fisher--matrix or Bayesian forecast, incorporating the actual source distribution and scanning law, is left to future work. The Python toolkit includes simple routines to compute $\hat{\alpha}(b)$ for given $(\alpha_{\rm Y},\lambda_{\rm Y})$ and to generate schematic forecast curves such as the one shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:yukawa}.

\section{Energy conditions and effective densities}
\label{sec:energy}

Given a phenomenological metric ansatz, an important question is whether the effective stress--energy tensor required to support it satisfies standard energy conditions. In the weak--field regime one may define an effective energy density $\rho_{\rm eff}(r)$ via a Poisson--like equation,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2 \Phi_{\rm eff}(r) \equiv \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{d\Phi_{\rm eff}}{dr}\right)
\simeq 4\pi G_0\,\rho_{\rm eff}(r),
\label{eq:poisson-eff}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_{\rm eff}$ is the potential entering $g_{tt}$, and we have neglected post--Newtonian corrections and pressure contributions. This approximation is valid provided $G_0M/(c^2r)\ll 1$ and $|\delta_\Phi(r)|\ll 1$; it is intended as a heuristic diagnostic rather than as a rigorous statement about the full non--linear solution.

For the Yukawa ansatz \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi}, applying \eqref{eq:poisson-eff} outside the point mass one finds that the Yukawa contribution to $\rho_{\rm eff}$ has the same sign as $\alpha_{\rm Y}$. In particular, for $\alpha_{\rm Y}>0$ the effective density is positive and the weak energy condition (WEC) is heuristically satisfied in the weak--field region, while for $\alpha_{\rm Y}<0$ it becomes negative, signaling a WEC violation. A more detailed expression is given in Appendix~A. This suggests imposing a phenomenological prior $\alpha_{\rm Y}\ge 0$ if one wishes to avoid such violations in the regime where the weak--field expansion is valid.

However, several caveats are in order. First, the true non--linear stress--energy tensor corresponding to a given metric ansatz may involve higher--derivative terms and effective pressures, so that simple WEC/DEC tests in the linearized regime are not decisive. Second, in an EFT with higher--curvature operators the appearance of negative effective energy densities in certain gauges does not necessarily imply pathologies; what matters for consistency is the absence of Ostrogradsky ghosts, superluminal propagation and other instabilities \cite{Stelle1978,Accioly2003,Burgess2004}. Finally, in a phenomenological framework such as ours we are not committing to a specific UV completion; we assume that any microscopic theory giving rise to the ans\"atze has a healthy spectrum of perturbations, and we use energy conditions as heuristic guides rather than as strict requirements (see also \cite{Wald1984,Visser1996}).

Figure~\ref{fig:rho-eff} illustrates the ratio $\rho_{\rm eff}(r)/\rho_{\rm GR}(r)$ for a solar--mass source with $\lambda_{\rm Y}=0.1$\,AU and three representative values of $\alpha_{\rm Y}$. For $\alpha_{\rm Y}>0$ the ratio remains close to unity in the radial range of interest, while for $\alpha_{\rm Y}<0$ it deviates significantly and becomes negative, reflecting the WEC violation in the linearized picture. At very large radii numerical round--off errors in $\rho_{\rm GR}$ amplify the ratio; these regions lie far outside the domain where the weak--field source interpretation is meaningful.

\section{Degeneracies, scheme dependence and model selection}
\label{sec:degeneracies}

An important limitation of any parameterization framework is the possibility of phenomenological degeneracies between different ans\"atze. Over a finite radial range, a Yukawa correction \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi} with $(\alpha_{\rm Y},\lambda_{\rm Y})$ can mimic a power--law correction \eqref{eq:powerlaw-Phi} with appropriate $(\epsilon,\beta,\ell_s)$ at the percent level, and an RG--improved ansatz \eqref{eq:G-of-r} can approximate Yukawa--like behavior for certain choices of $(\xi,n,r_0)$ and identification schemes $k(r)$. As a result, bounds obtained under one ansatz may not immediately translate into unique statements about underlying microscopic physics.

In this work we embrace this degeneracy to some extent and treat the benchmark ans\"atze as a set of ``basis functions'' spanning a class of possible deviations. Different probes at different scales constrain different linear combinations of these basis functions. Combining solar--system tests at $r\sim 1$--$10$\,AU with torsion--balance experiments at mm scales and GW observations at tens of km, for example, can break degeneracies between long--range and short--range corrections and between conservative and radiative sectors \cite{Berti2015,Clifton2012}.

A related issue is RG scheme dependence in the RG--improved ansatz. The identification between RG scale $k$ and geometric scale $r$ (or curvature $K$) is not unique and depends on truncation scheme, field content and renormalization prescription \cite{ReuterSaueressig2012}. Our parameterization \eqref{eq:G-of-r} should thus be viewed as a phenomenological description of a \emph{class} of possible RG--improved metrics rather than as a prediction of a specific truncation. Observational bounds on $(\xi,n,r_0)$ are, strictly speaking, bounds on the combination of running $G(k)$ and chosen $k(r)$ prescription. A systematic comparison of explicit schemes and their observational consequences will be presented in follow--up work.

In principle, one could use Bayesian model selection tools (Bayes factors, information criteria) to compare how well different ans\"atze fit multi--probe data. In practice this requires full likelihood analyses and a careful treatment of systematics, which goes beyond our current scope. Here we limit ourselves to emphasizing that multi--scale and multi--messenger observations are essential to discriminate between phenomenologically degenerate parameterizations.

\section{Discussion and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have presented an EFT--motivated framework for describing and testing scale--dependent modifications of static, spherically symmetric spacetime geometries. Within a weak--field expansion we constructed three benchmark ans\"atze (Yukawa, power--law, RG--improved), provided explicit mappings to PPN and ppE--like structures, and derived expressions for key observables such as perihelion precession, light deflection and Shapiro delay. We discussed how existing constraints from Cassini, LLR, torsion--balance experiments, binary pulsars and gravitational--wave observations constrain these parameterizations in different regimes, and we illustrated the potential of Gaia--like astrometry with a simple forecast.

The framework is deliberately conservative. We do not claim any detection of deviations from GR; all benchmark values and bounds are based on established experimental results and interpreted within clearly stated modeling assumptions (minimal ansatz, weak--field truncation, no screening). We explicitly acknowledge the limitations of the Poisson--like energy--density diagnostic, the truncation to leading order in $U/c^2$, and the model dependence of the mapping to ppE parameters.

Several directions for future work are natural:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A more systematic analysis of energy conditions and stability, including the spectrum of perturbations and possible ghost or Laplacian instabilities in specific UV completions (e.g.\ higher--derivative gravity \cite{Stelle1978,Accioly2003}, scalar--tensor theories \cite{Freire2012}, nonlocal models).
\item A detailed comparison of different RG improvement schemes, including curvature--based identifications $k^4\sim K$ and their impact on observables; this will sharpen the connection to asymptotically safe gravity.
\item Full Bayesian global fits combining solar--system, binary--pulsar and gravitational--wave data in the unified parameter spaces of our ans\"atze, including screening mechanisms where relevant \cite{Clifton2012,Joyce2015}.
\item Extensions to rotating (Kerr--like) spacetimes and to cosmological backgrounds, where scale--dependent modifications may leave imprints on strong lensing, black--hole shadows and large--scale structure.
\end{itemize}

With ongoing and upcoming experiments such as Gaia DR4/DR5, future astrometric missions, third--generation GW detectors (Einstein Telescope, Cosmic Explorer), improved LLR and torsion--balance measurements, and satellite missions testing the equivalence principle, the next decade will provide a wealth of data to probe gravity at multiple scales. We hope that the framework and toolkit presented here will be useful to both theorists and experimentalists in designing and interpreting such tests.

\section*{Appendix A: Effective densities for the Yukawa ansatz}

In this appendix we sketch the computation of the effective density $\rho_{\rm eff}(r)$ associated with the Yukawa ansatz, within the Poisson--like approximation \eqref{eq:poisson-eff}. Writing
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\rm eff}(r) = -\frac{G_0M}{r}\Bigl[1 + \alpha_{\rm Y} e^{-r/\lambda_{\rm Y}}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
and applying $\nabla^2$ in spherical symmetry yields
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2 \Phi_{\rm eff} = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{d\Phi_{\rm eff}}{dr}\right)
= 4\pi G_0 M\,\delta^{(3)}(\bm{r}) + \nabla^2 \Phi_{\rm Y}(r),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_{\rm Y}$ denotes the Yukawa part and we have isolated the point--mass source. A straightforward calculation gives, for $r>0$,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2 \Phi_{\rm Y}(r) = -4\pi G_0 \rho_{\rm Y}(r),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\rm Y}(r) = -\frac{\alpha_{\rm Y} M}{4\pi \lambda_{\rm Y}^2}\,
\frac{e^{-r/\lambda_{\rm Y}}}{r}.
\end{equation}
Thus, in the weak--field Poisson picture the Yukawa correction corresponds to an effective density shell of sign opposite to $\alpha_{\rm Y}$ surrounding the point mass. For $\alpha_{\rm Y}>0$ this is positive; for $\alpha_{\rm Y}<0$ it is negative, signaling a heuristic WEC violation outside the source. As emphasized in Sec.~\ref{sec:energy}, this statement is limited to the linearized regime and does not account for pressure or higher--derivative contributions.

\section*{Appendix B: Light deflection integral}

Here we briefly outline the derivation of the deflection integral \eqref{eq:deflection-general}. For the metric \eqref{eq:metric} with \eqref{eq:PhiPsi-def}, the null geodesic equations can be reduced, in the weak--field limit, to an effective refractive index depending on $\Phi+\Psi$ \cite{Will2014}. To leading order in $U/c^2$ one finds
\begin{equation}
\hat{\alpha}(b) \simeq \frac{2}{c^2} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigl[\Phi(r)+\Psi(r)\bigr]\frac{b\,dz}{r},
\end{equation}
with $r=\sqrt{b^2+z^2}$. For GR, substituting $\Phi=\Psi=-U(r)$ reproduces $\hat{\alpha}_{\rm GR}=4G_0M/(c^2 b)$. For the Yukawa ansatz one may substitute \eqref{eq:yukawa-Phi} and either evaluate the integral numerically or, in certain limits, approximate the exponential factor to extract analytic estimates. The Python toolkit includes a numerical implementation based on adaptive quadrature together with a GR validation test.
